Key takeaways

  • In Jun 2026, 2.40% of active loans were 30+ days past due and 0.74% were 90+ (peak 90+: 3.87% in Feb 2021).
  • Every vintage peaks in 2020, at a different loan age. Loan age alone does not explain the timing.
  • Of 112,768 loans that reached 90+ days, 2.7% ended in a credit-event exit; 38% are current again and 48% prepaid or matured.
  • From July 2023, credit scores below 700 were 16% of loan-months but 39% of defaults.

Definitions

Delinquency buckets use months past due as reported: 30-59, 60-89, 90-179 and 180+ days. Default is the first month at 180+ days or an exit coded 02, 03, 09 or 15. The clean cohort is loans first seen within 3 months of origination (98.0% of loans). Balances are masked by the source for the first six months, so rates are loan-count based.

Forecast method

Three methods share one exposure projection: a run-rate benchmark, an age-only curve, and an age x calendar model (age effect x calendar effect x vintage level, fitted jointly). The age-only curve is excluded from the chart because it fails after the 2020-21 shift.

Limits

Four first-quarter cohorts only; counts of loans, not dollar losses; the forecast assumes recent calendar conditions persist.
